RigIQ

Equipment tracking, maintenance records and an equipment-sharing marketplace, built for the indigenous service companies that keep Nigeria's oil and gas operations running.

The problem

Indigenous O&G service companies run fleets of costly equipment. Maintenance history usually sits in scattered Excel files and in people's heads. When a machine fails unexpectedly, the job stops and the company pays for it. Global maintenance software (CMMS) isn't built around how these companies work or what they can pay. I couldn't find a Nigerian-built option for this segment.

What I built
  • An equipment register and dashboard so every asset, its status and its service history live in one place
  • Maintenance tracking built from real machine and service records
  • A marketplace where companies can lease idle equipment to each other, with flexible payment terms such as paying after the job
  • A public site at rigiq.ng, built and deployed by me
Result so far
  • ARCO signed on as the first pilot client (Oct 2026) and loaded 97 machines
  • Early testers include NDT technicians and engineers who signed up through the RigIQ form
  • Selected for the iDICE Founders Lab and as a top-5 pitch finalist
  • Pricing: free for up to 10 assets, with a ₦75,000/month professional tier

Where it's going

The next step is predictive maintenance. RigIQ will use sensor data from machines to flag failures before they happen. Large operators (IOCs) are the first target for that, and the go-to-market plan includes working through the Maintenance Institute (Nigeria) to reach the companies it already services.
